{# IS A LOWER PRICE A RED FLAG #}

Is a Lower Botox Price a Red Flag?

Not on its own. A lower price does not mean an unsafe treatment, and a higher one does not guarantee a better result; in a market where {{ obs.cluster_pct }}% of listings sit in a narrow band, small differences say little.

A price far below the rest is a reasonable prompt to ask who administers the injections, whether they are licensed and trained, and whether the product comes from an authorized source. The FDA has warned about counterfeit botulinum toxin from unauthorized distributors. The goal is not to avoid low prices, but to understand why a price is where it is before booking.

{# 3 QUESTIONS TO ASK #}

3 Questions to Ask Before Booking

1. How many units does this price include? A treatment-area total means little without the dose. Two providers can quote the same price for very different amounts of product, so this is the single most useful check before comparing quotes.

2. Which product is it? Not every listing advertising "Botox" uses the same neuromodulator. Ask for the specific product name so you are comparing like with like.

3. What happens if I need a follow-up? Similar upfront prices can hide different touch-up policies. Knowing that up front makes the comparison cleaner.
