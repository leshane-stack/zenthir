Methodology

Zenthir is healthcare pricing intelligence infrastructure. Here is exactly how we collect, verify, and present data.

Data Sources

Pricing data comes from multiple sources, each with different levels of reliability. We never mix source types without labeling them.

CMS Medicare Provider Charge Data, CMS publishes what every Medicare-billing provider charges (submitted charges) and what Medicare pays (allowed amounts) for each procedure. This covers hospitals, physicians, and outpatient facilities nationwide. This is our highest-confidence source with nearly 6 million pricing records.

CMS Hospital Price Transparency Files, US hospitals are legally required to publish machine-readable pricing files under the Hospital Price Transparency Rule. We ingest these files directly from hospital websites and CMS registries for hospital and imaging center pricing.

Provider-Reported Data, Providers who claim their Zenthir profile can submit and update their own pricing. This data is labeled as provider-reported and carries high confidence because the provider has a direct incentive for accuracy.

Market Estimates, For cash-pay providers where no public pricing data exists (plastic surgery, dental, fertility, med spas), we generate estimated pricing based on regional market ranges, published fee schedules, and aggregated public sources. These are clearly labeled as estimates with medium confidence.

Understanding Pricing Data

Healthcare pricing has multiple layers. We display these clearly so you can interpret the data correctly.

Submitted Charges (Billed Amount), What the provider bills for a procedure. This is typically the highest number and represents the chargemaster or sticker price. Cash-pay patients without negotiated rates may be charged near this amount, though most providers offer cash-pay discounts.

Medicare Allowed Amount, What Medicare determines is reasonable for a procedure in a given area. This is typically much lower than the submitted charge and represents a benchmark for fair pricing. Many providers accept this amount from Medicare patients and may offer cash-pay rates in a similar range.

Provider-Confirmed Price, What a claimed provider reports as their actual cash-pay price. This is the most directly useful data point for patients paying out of pocket.

Why this matters: When our Price Check tool compares your bill against our data, it uses submitted charges and Medicare rates as benchmarks. If you paid significantly more than what providers typically submit for the same procedure, that is a strong signal of overcharging. If you paid more than the Medicare allowed amount but less than the submitted charge, your price may be reasonable but worth investigating.

Regional Median Comparison

When you see "vs Regional Median" on a provider page, this compares the provider's price for a specific procedure against the median price across all providers in the same metropolitan area offering that procedure. This helps you understand whether a provider is priced above or below the local market.

Medians require at least 3 providers in a region to be calculated. With fewer data points, we do not display a comparison to avoid misleading precision.

Update Frequency

CMS hospital pricing files are re-ingested weekly. Provider-reported data updates immediately when submitted. Market estimates are refreshed monthly. Every data point displays a "last verified" date so you can judge recency yourself.

What We Do Not Do

Rankings and pricing positions are never influenced by payment. Providers who claim profiles get operational tools and verification, not ranking advantages. We do not accept payment to alter, remove, or reposition pricing data. The data layer and the monetization layer are structurally independent.

We do not provide medical advice. Zenthir is pricing and provider intelligence infrastructure, not a clinical recommendation engine.

Limitations

Healthcare pricing is complex. A single procedure can vary based on anesthesia, facility fees, implants, follow-up care, and insurance negotiation. The prices shown on Zenthir represent the base procedure cost as reported or estimated. Actual out-of-pocket costs may differ.

Market estimates carry inherent uncertainty. We label them clearly so you can weigh the data appropriately.

If you believe any data on Zenthir is inaccurate, providers can submit corrections through the profile claim process. We investigate every correction request.
